# Initial Playtest Log

## November 26 · Developer self-playtest

**Session.** Developer self-playtest, reported by Hao in Chinese. One designer, zero external participants. The account describes the local four-night prototype. Exact build, farmer mode and lamp action IDs were not captured. Route below is recalled, without a telemetry file.

| Night | Entrance | Reported destination and outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | West | Brought home the gnome near the west entrance. Recalled leaving footprints and seeing the farmer work on a lamp. |
| 2 | West | Returned through the familiar entrance for the central gnome. The lamp left the approach usable. |
| 3 | West | Used the west entrance again to retrieve the gnome by the farmhouse door. |
| 4 | East | Retrieved the nearby eastern gnome. The light narrowed the approach enough to make Hao consider the small gap at the edge. |

**What the run shows.** Hao could use the same entrance for three different targets. He considered the eastern light when choosing where to step. The recalled hammering establishes what Hao saw. The exact operation and which trace the farmer inspected require a recorded plan.

**Design interpretation.** Hao knew to bring each gnome back to a hedge entrance. He judged that the opening gives a newcomer little reason to discover that objective. First-time comprehension has not been observed. He also wanted more exploration and anticipation when choosing a route.

**Proposed next change.** In the first-night entrance view, show a small illustrated sequence of a gnome on a pedestal, the gardener carrying it, and the gardener returning through a hedge opening. Reuse the same hedge symbol when carrying the first gnome. Leave the location of the next gnome for the player to discover. Hao prefers visual guidance. This specific treatment awaits his decision.

**Next test.** Can a first-time player explain the goal and return one gnome before receiving a hint? For defense placement, compare the path before and after a lamp change at the same landmark. Pair the tile sequence with the player's stated reason for staying or turning. The external session documented below followed this self-test.

## External playtest 01 · Notes prepared November 28

**Question.** Can a newcomer tell how to bring a gnome home, and does seeing a farmer response affect what they expect on the next night?

**Method.** One anonymous external player played the local prototype while Hao facilitated in Chinese. Before play, Hao explained the theft objective, the three hedge openings and the two-point stamina budget. Hao confirmed that the player agreed before play to the use of anonymous feedback in coursework. A design discussion followed the session. Exact routes, stamina balances and final score were not verified against a trace.

| Moment | What the player said or did | What we learned |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Choosing an entrance | Asked whether West, East and South Hedge were different maps. After Hao explained that all three openings lead into the same farm, the player described them as extraction points. | The entrance labels alone left the relationship between the choices unclear. |
| Finding a gnome | Asked what the first item was. Hao identified it as a map. Later, the player recognized a gnome but asked where to put it. Hao prompted them to take it back. | Recognition and delivery are separate steps. Even after the goal briefing, the destination needed a hint. Unaided comprehension of the opening remains untested. |
| Spending stamina | After saying they had closed something that was already open, the player asked whether they could still pick up a gnome. | The player connected a previous interaction to a possible loss of options. The exact interaction cost is unknown. |
| Seeing a new lamp | The player noticed the farmer lighting a lamp, asked whether the farmer would chase them and whether they could outrun him, then asked if they had just been caught. Hao explained that capture forfeits that night’s gnome. | The lamp drew attention. The player was still working out when detection took effect and whether escape was possible. Their next route choice is unknown. |

**Debrief.** The player liked searching for gnomes and felt the camera showed enough of the small farm. They asked what collecting gnomes would earn. After Hao explained how traces inform morning defenses, they likened the exchange to a game of chess. The chase expectation arose during play. Hao later explicitly invited chase ideas, which led to suggestions about sound, a barking dog and a searching farmer. The follow-up discussion also raised the limited number of interactions.

**Proposed follow-up.** Preserve the search. On the first pickup, try a small hedge-opening symbol beside the carried gnome and repeat it at an exit. With a fresh player, record whether they bring the gnome home before Hao gives a destination hint. At the first detection, ask what just happened and what they can do next. Use that answer to decide whether to clarify the capture feedback before considering a chase. These proposals await Hao’s decision.
